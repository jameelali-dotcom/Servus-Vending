Placement agreement — what to cover

Even informal arrangements benefit from being written down, one to two pages, kept collaborative
  • Parties and the exact location / address
  • Term & termination notice (e.g. ongoing, 30 days either way)
  • What you'll provide — machine type, servicing frequency, product range
  • Responsibility for upkeep, cleanliness, and damage
  • How you'll access the machine for servicing — hours, keys, escort
  • A contact point on both sides for day-to-day issues

How too's

SECTION 04.1–04.4
4 operational areas
1Open & inspect interior — spills, debris, pest signs, odours
2Check for expired or near-expiry products; rotate first-in, first-out
3Restock to par levels based on what's sold since last visit
4Inspect for jammed trays, misaligned coils, or stuck items
5Clean exterior glass, buttons, and the surrounding area; empty nearby bins
6Test coin/cash mechanism — acceptance and correct change
7Collect cash, count and log on the spot, secure in a lockbox/sealed bag
8Verify the machine locks properly before leaving
9Speak briefly with the site contact for feedback or issues
10Log the visit — restocked items, cash collected, issues found/fixed
Par levels

ensure items are at baseline quantities.

Per-visit log

Paper, spreadsheet, or notes app — record what sold (what's missing) and what was restocked, every visit.

Visit frequency

Every Week , then reviewed based on visit log

Stock rotation

First-in, first-out stacking. expiry dates checked .

Routine upkeep — every visit
  • Exterior & interior cleaning, including buttons, glass, and surrounding area
  • Clearing the coin/cash mechanism of dust, debris, or jams
  • Visual check for loose panels, damaged decals, or signs of tampering
Scheduled preventive maintenance
Monthly
  • Deeper clean of coin mechanism & bill path
  • Check & tighten loose fittings
  • Check lighting, replace bulbs
Quarterly
  • Inspect product delivery mechanisms (coils, trays) for wear and calibration
  • Check the machine is level and securely positioned
  • Review surroundings for pest activity
As needed
  • Replace worn parts with manufacturer-recommended or compatible parts
  • Address persistent jams, mis-vends, or mechanical noise immediately
Maintenance log: record every repair, part replaced, and recurring issue per machine. Patterns here — like the same coil jamming repeatedly — tell you when a part, or the whole machine, needs replacing, and support warranty or insurance claims.
Ensure each machine is stable, level, and anchored if the manufacturer recommends it.
Position machines in visible, supervised areas where possible — visibility is the cheapest theft deterrent.
Use high-security locks; upgrade factory locks if a location has any history of tampering.
Avoid leaving large cash amounts in the machine for extended periods — collect on a schedule matched to typical takings.
Keep a record of serial numbers, lock/key details, and machine condition (photos) for insurance purposes.
